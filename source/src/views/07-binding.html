Binding, tucking and packing

For many people these everyday practices make it possible to leave the house feeling like themselves. They bring real relief and some real wear on the body. Here is how they affect you, and how to make them gentler.

Chest binding

Binding means flattening the chest with a tight garment, usually a purpose-made . In a US survey of 13- to 24-year-olds, people who bound were misgendered less often, and across studies people who bind describe less chest dysphoria and better mood.

What binding does to breathing

When you breathe in, the diaphragm, the dome of muscle under the lungs, flattens downward. At the same time the lower ribs swing out to the sides like the handle of a bucket, and the upper ribs and breastbone lift forward like an old water-pump handle. A tight binder holds the ribs in, so each breath is shallower and the muscles of the neck and shoulders work harder to help. That extra work is one reason binding can bring neck, shoulder and upper-back pain.

Gentler binding

There are no trials of safer binding, so this advice comes from the one large survey and from clinicians' and community experience.

  1. Bind fewer days when you can, and take the binder off for breaks during the day.
  2. Choose your true size, never smaller. If you can't take a full, comfortable breath, or it hurts, it's too tight.
  3. Don't sleep in it. Your ribs, skin and lungs need the night off.
  4. Swap it for exercise. A sports compression top or similar is gentler when you're breathing hard.
  5. Skip bandages, tape and plastic wrap. They aren't made for binding, can tighten as you move and can damage skin.
  6. Look after your skin. Keep it dry, wash the binder often, and give rashes or broken skin time to heal.
  7. Get help early for pain that lasts, rib or chest pain, numbness, breathlessness, or skin that breaks down. You won't be told off.
A five-minute binder breakGentle movements for when the binder comes off

General movements, not a treatment. Stop anything that hurts, and ask a physiotherapist or osteopath to tailor them for you.

  1. Side-rib breathing. Rest your hands on your lower ribs. Breathe in slowly through your nose and feel the ribs widen into your hands; breathe out slowly. Five breaths.
  2. Doorway chest opener. Forearms on a door frame at shoulder height, step gently forward until you feel a stretch across the front of the chest. Hold 20 to 30 seconds.
  3. Shoulder-blade squeeze. Draw your shoulder blades back and down, hold for 5 seconds, relax. Ten times.
  4. Upper-back extension. Sitting tall, clasp your hands behind your head and gently lift your breastbone toward the ceiling. Five slow repeats.
  5. Neck release. Slowly tip one ear toward the shoulder, breathe out, and come back. Three each side.

Tucking

positions the penis and testes back between the legs for a flatter look, often with a gaff (firm, close-fitting underwear) or tape. Reported problems include itching, skin irritation and infections, and, rarely, testicular torsion: a testis twisting on its cord, which is an emergency. Extensive tucking has also been linked with lower sperm quality.

  • A gaff or tucking underwear is gentler on skin than tape. If you use tape, use skin-safe medical tape, never duct tape, and remove it slowly.
  • Take breaks, keep the area clean and dry, and don't hold on when you need to pass urine.
  • If you might want to store sperm, pausing tucking for three to four months beforehand may help.

Sudden, severe pain or swelling in a testis needs emergency care now. Untuck, and go to an emergency department or call 000. Testicular torsion needs treatment within hours.
