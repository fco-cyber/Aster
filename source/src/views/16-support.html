Help, now and later

Getting help

People and services that understand gender diversity, from crisis lines to clinics, peer groups and legal help. They follow where you live; choose another place if this one isn't yours.

000

If you or someone else is in danger, or has chest pain, sudden breathlessness or signs of a stroke, call 000.

Who does what

GP (family doctor)Often the first step, and the long-term anchor

Many GPs start and monitor hormones using informed consent, organise blood tests, screening and referrals, and look after the rest of your health.

EndocrinologistHormone specialist

A specialist in hormones, for more complex situations, and for the one-off second opinion needed for PBS-subsidised testosterone (a urologist or sexual health physician can also give this).

Psychologist or counsellorSupport, not gatekeeping

For support with dysphoria, anxiety, depression, relationships or the stress of prejudice, and for help thinking through decisions. Your GP can refer you for Medicare-subsidised sessions.

Speech pathologistVoice training

Helps you find a voice that feels like yours, safely, and helps with voice strain on testosterone.

Physiotherapist or osteopathMuscles, joints, posture and recovery

For pain from binding, recovering movement after top surgery, training safely as strength changes, and hypermobile joints. Pelvic health physiotherapists help before and after genital surgery and with pelvic pain.

Fertility clinicStoring sperm or eggs

Talk about storing sperm or eggs before starting hormones or before surgery that removes the testes or ovaries.

Peer supportPeople who've been there

Other trans people can offer understanding no clinician can, and in a US survey, support from them softened the link between stigma and distress.

About Aster

Aster is general information for trans and gender diverse adults in Australia, and the people who support them, written in plain English and reviewed on 21 November 2026. It isn't medical advice and can't take your own health history into account; please talk with your doctor about your own care. In an emergency, call 000.
